Colorful partition

Splitting the liver volume into connected compartments that contain terminal nodes of every tree

Mariia Myshkina, Matthias König

2026-11-26

The question

What do we have?

  • a closed surface mesh of the volume \(V\) (liver)
  • \(N = 4n \approx 400\,000\) terminal nodes, 4 colours (trees A, P, V, B), \(n \approx 100\,000\) per colour
  • now: every terminal gets the same share of the volume

→

What is asked?

Split \(V\) into cells so that every cell

  1. contains at least one point of each colour
  2. is one connected region inside \(V\)
  3. is compact, roughly round (Voronoi-like)

and return the volume of every cell.

→

What for?

  • one cell = one sinusoid compartment that couples all four trees
  • compartment volume from the real geometry instead of an equal split

Why is it hard?

\(V\) is not a box

  • the first specification used Delaunay + mirror points across the faces of a box
  • exact only for convex volumes with flat faces
  • in a non-convex \(V\) (e.g. a U-shape), Voronoi neighbours can touch only outside \(V\) → “connected in the graph” ≠ “connected in space”

No package in Julia

  • what is needed: a 3D Voronoi diagram restricted to a mesh (restricted Voronoi diagram)
  • Julia: Voronoi in 2D only, Delaunay in 3D — no restricted 3D Voronoi
  • exists in C++ (Geogram) → would need a C wrapper, its own build and maintenance

Colours and size

  • every cell must have all 4 colours — a constraint across groups of points, not only distances
  • every cell must stay connected while points are moved between cells
  • \(N \approx 400\,000\) points, \(\approx 11\) million voxels → must run in about a minute

Three options

A · Mirror points

Delaunay of the points + their mirror images across the faces of \(V\)

  • exact volumes
  • only for convex polyhedra ✗

B · Voxels ✓ chosen

Fill \(V\) with small cubes, give each cube to its nearest point

  • pure Julia, any shape
  • connections never pass through the outside
  • volumes ≈ 1 % per final cell (estimate)

C · Restricted Voronoi (Geogram, C++)

Exact cells of every point, cut to \(V\)

  • exact volumes
  • C++ library, wrapper never compiled
  • kept as a fallback if exact volumes are needed

Central idea of B: every point owns the part of \(V\) closest to it (its small cell). A final cell is a group of points → after the voxel step, everything is done on a graph.

Pipeline

Step What Result
0 · 1a voxel size, inside test, nearest point per voxel owner
1c · 1b · 1d · 1e one piece per small cell, volumes, neighbour graph, points without voxels graph
2 · 3 seeds, grow cells on the graph label
4 · 5 · 6 find cells missing a colour, repair, smooth all cells colourful
7 · 8 · 9 label map, cell volumes, checks cells + volumes

The graph ignores colours — neighbours are defined only by touching in space. Colours enter only in steps 4–6.

Steps 0 · 1a — grid, inside test, owners

0 Voxel grid

  • mean spacing \(s = (\mathrm{vol}(V)/N)^{1/3}\)
  • voxel size \(h = s/3\) → ≈ 27 voxels per point

1a Inside test

  • per column: where does it cross the surface?
  • crossings alternate enter / leave
  • odd number → hole in the mesh, column skipped

1a Owners

  • every inside voxel → its nearest point (KD-tree)
  • the voxels of a point = its small cell

Steps 1c · 1b — one piece per small cell, volumes

1c Before

  • near thin parts, a point can own voxels on both sides of a gap
  • its small cell has two pieces

1c After

  • each point keeps its home piece
  • a stray piece goes to the neighbour it shares the most faces with

1b Small-cell volumes (after 1c)

  • voxels owned × \(h^3\)
  • sum ≈ \(\mathrm{vol}(V)\)

Steps 1d · 1e — the neighbour graph

1d Neighbour graph

  • two points are neighbours if their voxels share a face
  • outside voxels belong to nobody → no edge through the outside
  • colours are ignored

1e Points without voxels

  • a very close neighbour wins all their voxels
  • one edge to the nearest point that has voxels; its colour still counts

Step 2 — seeds

2 Random seeds

  • \(m \approx n/4\) random data points
  • ≈ 16 points, ≈ 4 of each colour per cell

2 Lloyd rounds

  • 3–5 rounds: points → nearest seed, seed → mean of its points
  • even spacing → cells of similar size

2 Snap

  • every seed → its nearest data point, duplicates dropped
  • a mean can fall outside \(V\)

Steps 3 · 4 — grow cells, count colours

3 Growing

  • multi-source Dijkstra on the graph
  • edge length \(\lVert p_i - p_j \rVert\)

3 Grown

  • every point takes the label of the seed closest along the graph
  • every cell is connected and compact

4 Count colours

  • points of each colour per cell
  • a cell missing a colour is deficient (≈ 2 % per colour expected)

Steps 5 · 6 — repair and smoothing

5 Path to the missing colour

  • search outward from the deficient cell for the nearest point of the missing colour
  • accepted only if every cell losing points keeps all colours and stays connected

5 Repaired

  • the path joins the cell
  • no acceptable path after ~20 tries → merge with a neighbouring cell

6 Smoothing (optional)

  • a boundary point moves to the cell with the closer centre
  • only if both cells stay valid

Steps 7 · 8 · 9 — result and checks

7 Label map

  • every voxel gets the cell of its owner
  • 3D: an image of the cells (VTK)

8 Cell volumes

  • sum of the small-cell volumes of the cell’s points

9 Checks

  • every cell has all colours
  • every cell is one piece in space (flood fill over its voxels)
  • \(\sum\) cell volumes \(= (\text{inside voxels}) \cdot h^3 \approx \mathrm{vol}(V)\)
  • accuracy: rerun with \(h/1.5\), compare cell volumes

Status and next steps

  • expected cost for \(N = 400\,000\): about a minute, memory < 1 GB (start Julia with -t auto)
